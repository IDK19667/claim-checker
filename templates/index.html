                How a check works

                1. Your claim becomes a search. An AI model turns "apple cider vinegar cures diabetes" into the terms researchers actually use.
                2. PubMed answers. That's the U.S. National Library of Medicine's index of medical research, 37 million papers. We take up to 8 of the best matches with their abstracts.
                3. The studies get weighed, not counted. A meta-analysis of many trials outranks one small pilot. A case report about one patient barely counts. The model reads the abstracts and says which studies it relied on.
                4. The verdict is stamped, with rules. "False" needs studies that contradict the claim. Nothing cited means "it's complicated," never a confident answer. Studies that only match the words don't count.
                5. Every verdict says what's still open. Research is rarely finished. The last line names the gap: who the studies left out, how short they ran, or what finding would change the answer.

                Reading the evidence meter

                • Strong: the verdict rests on a meta-analysis, systematic review, or randomized trial.
                • Moderate: human studies or expert reviews, but no randomized trial behind it.
                • Weak: only case reports, letters, or opinion pieces.
                • No direct evidence: nothing found actually tests the claim.

                Not medical advice. The model can misread an abstract, and PubMed doesn't index everything. Read the studies. Talk to a clinician about your own health.
